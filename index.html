Water rights and drought

Ninth Circuit says California owed water rights holders a hearing before its 2021 drought cutoffs

A unanimous panel revived due process claims over curtailments on two Sacramento River tributaries, a ruling that could reshape how the state handles the next dry year.

A unanimous Ninth Circuit panel on November 24 reinstated due process claims brought by Stanford Vina Ranch Irrigation, Los Molinos Mutual Water and Peyton Pacific, which hold most of the water rights on Deer Creek and Mill Creek, two Sacramento River tributaries in Tehama County. They challenged the State Water Resources Control Board's 2021 emergency curtailment, which stopped diversions by 22 rights holders irrigating some 11,000 acres of orchards and pasture after Governor Gavin Newsom declared a drought emergency and agencies moved to protect salmon and steelhead.

A Sacramento trial judge had treated the emergency regulations and curtailment orders as legislative, which requires no individual hearing. Writing for the panel, Judge Lawrence VanDyke found them adjudicatory because they targeted a small, identifiable group of rights holders, so those holders were owed notice and a meaningful hearing before losing the right to divert. He wrote that the distinction "turns on the character of the action, rather than its label." The plaintiffs said they got a five minute public comment period and no chance to cross examine the state's witnesses.

In a separate memorandum the panel upheld the dismissal of the companies' takings claims, noting that compensation can be pursued through an inverse condemnation suit in California state court. The ruling does not change current water allocations, and the case now proceeds on the revived claims. The plaintiffs' attorney said the state has stopped their diversions four times in seven years; the Attorney General's office did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Sources: Courthouse News via Maven's Notebook, Nov 25, 2026; Ninth Circuit opinion via Justia, Nov 24, 2026; Wisevoter, Nov 25, 2026

CapitalUnited States

Sciens Water buys Roberts Filter Group, a filtration maker founded in 1889, as its third platform

Sciens Water Opportunities Fund II closed its purchase of Roberts Filter Group, a Coatesville, Pennsylvania maker of gravity filters, underdrains and air scour systems, on November 9 and announced it on November 12. Family owned for more than a century, the company reports more than 4,500 filtration systems installed on six continents and more than 35 active patents, with long standing customers including Baltimore, Philadelphia and Puerto Rico's water authority. Matt Roberts stays on as chief executive. The announcement gave no price.

Why it matters. Engineered filtration for large utilities joins Sciens's regulated utility and decentralized treatment platforms, a bet on steady municipal plant spending.

Sources: Smart Water Magazine, Nov 18, 2026; PR Newswire via Yahoo Finance, Nov 12, 2026

RegulationUnited Nations

UN General Assembly makes the water conference a fixture, every three years from 2031

The General Assembly on November 23 adopted by consensus a resolution tabled by the UAE and Senegal that holds a UN Water Conference every three years starting in 2031, after the meetings already agreed for 2026 and 2028. The Secretary-General is asked to report at least six months before the 2031 conference, and the meetings are financed outside the regular UN budget. The next conference, co-hosted by the UAE and Senegal, runs December 8 to 10 in Abu Dhabi. Before 2023, the UN had held only one, in Mar del Plata in 1977.

Why it matters. A standing calendar gives governments, development banks and suppliers a regular deadline for water commitments and financing.

Sources: The National, Nov 23, 2026; The National, Nov 25, 2026

TechnologyWyoming

Cheyenne's utility says data centers took about 2% of its water sales in 2025

The Cheyenne Board of Public Utilities sold about 3.5 billion gallons of water in 2025, and 93 million gallons went to data centers, Frank Strong, its engineering and water resource division manager, told Cowboy State Daily. He said actual use came in well below what operators first proposed, as newer chips can run at higher temperatures and need less water based cooling.

Why it matters. Metered utility data, not early projections, is starting to anchor the data center water debate. Drier basins will still want campus by campus numbers.

Sources: Cowboy State Daily, Nov 25, 2026

UtilitiesUnited Kingdom

UK says water supply no longer stalls any planned Greater Cambridge development

A progress report from the housing and environment ministries, published November 20, said the Environment Agency has withdrawn objections raised since 2022 to more than 9,000 homes and 500,000 square meters of commercial space, after Cambridge Water updated its water resources plan in March 2025. The package includes 2,048 social homes retrofitted for water efficiency, a Grafham to Cambridge transfer of up to 26 megaliters a day from 2032, and the proposed Fens Reservoir at up to 87 megaliters a day, which still needs a Development Consent Order.

Why it matters. Water has become a gating item for housing and lab space. Cambridge shows efficiency, metering and transfers unlocking approvals before new reservoirs arrive.

Sources: Smart Water Magazine, Nov 24, 2026

Innovation of the weekWeek of November 23

A Rochester lab desalinates seawater without leaving brine behind

~100%Dissolved salts recovered as solids
~50%Lithium recovered from leftover salts

Most desalination plants send concentrated brine back to the sea. A team led by Chunlei Guo at the University of Rochester's Institute of Optics built a solar thermal system that uses no chemical additives and discharges no liquid brine, extracting nearly 100% of the dissolved salts in solid form. It tested real seawater from the Pacific, Atlantic and Indian Oceans and published the work in Light: Science & Applications. A related study recovered about half the lithium in salts left after desalination, using Great Salt Lake samples. The work is at laboratory stage, and the release gives no flow rate or cost figures.

Sources: ScienceDaily, Sep 16, 2026

Supplier of the weekWeek of November 23

Water treatment chemistry

Kemira is buying its way into industrial water services while raw material costs squeeze margins

Kemira, the Helsinki based maker of coagulants, flocculants and other chemicals for water intensive industries, employs about 4,900 people. On October 23 it reported third quarter revenue of €709 million, up 3%, with flat organic growth and about €18 million added by acquisitions. Chief executive Antti Salminen kept full year guidance of €2.6 to €3.0 billion in revenue and €400 to €500 million in operative EBITDA.

Water Solutions, its largest segment, grew revenue 5% to €331 million, mainly through acquisitions, with a 19.7% operative EBITDA margin. Kemira closed the purchase of Nebraska based Water Engineering, an industrial water treatment services firm serving food and beverage, manufacturing and healthcare, in October 2025, then added ANMAC Water Treatment Service and Italy's REPUR, which provides activated carbon services, in the third quarter.

The pressure is on costs. Group operative EBITDA margin fell to 15.9% from 20.0% as variable costs rose €35 million against €11 million of price increases, and Kemira puts the gross 2026 cost impact of the Middle East conflict at about €100 million, roughly half of it mitigated. On November 23 it said it will invest in a venture fund to back startup innovation.

Sources: Investing.com, Oct 23, 2026; Kemira, Oct 24, 2025; Kemira; GuruFocus via Yahoo Finance, Oct 24, 2026

Istanbul, Türkiye

Basilica Cistern

A sixth century reservoir held up by a forest of 336 columns

Built between 532 and 542 under the Eastern Roman emperor Justinian I, the Basilica Cistern, Yerebatan Sarnıcı in Turkish, was one of the city's major water stores, with an estimated capacity of about 100,000 tons. Its roof rests on 336 columns, each 9 meters tall.

Two carved Medusa heads, identified in 1986, serve as column bases, one upside down and one on its side. The cistern opened to visitors in 1987 after a major restoration, and reopened on July 22, 2022 after a second restoration that began in 2014.

Why it matters. Istanbul hosts the IWRA World Water Congress in October 2027, and this is the city's oldest lesson in storing water close to demand.

If you go. In Istanbul's historic peninsula; open as a museum. Check opening hours and tickets before you go.
