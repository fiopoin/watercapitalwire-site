Policy and capital

Abu Dhabi will put water investment on the UN agenda for the first time since 2023

The UN Water Conference runs December 8 to 10. It will end with a President's summary, not a negotiated text, so the deals signed around it will matter more than the communiqué.

The 2026 United Nations Water Conference, co-hosted by the United Arab Emirates and Senegal, convenes at the Abu Dhabi National Exhibition Centre from December 8 to 10. It is the first UN conference dedicated to water since the 2023 meeting in New York, which was itself the first in nearly half a century. Its stated purpose is to accelerate progress on Sustainable Development Goal 6, clean water and sanitation for all.

Member states adopted six themes for the interactive dialogues in July 2025. One of them, investments for water, is the reason capital providers should be watching. The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs says the opening day is framed around water for shared prosperity and the closing day around a resilient future built together.

The format sets expectations. IUCN has noted that the conference will produce a summary by the President of the General Assembly instead of a negotiated outcome document. That shifts attention to what gets signed on the margins: sovereign desalination and reuse programs, utility financing frameworks and partnerships with development banks. SIWI has already said World Water Week 2027 will follow up on the commitments made in Abu Dhabi.

Sources: UN DESA; UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs; IUCN; SIWI

Across the water economy

The deals, contracts and rules that moved since the last edition.

CapitalUnited States

US water utilities keep consolidating as American Water and Essential near the finish line

Pennsylvania American Water closed its $32.8 million purchase of the Indian Creek Valley Water Authority system on November 18, adding about 2,600 connections and promising $9 million of upgrades over five years. The larger deal is still moving: Illinois became the fourth state to approve the American Water and Essential Utilities merger, announced November 6, with a Pennsylvania settlement pending approval.

Why it matters. The all-stock merger would create a utility worth about $40 billion serving more than 4.7 million connections, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2027. Small municipal systems remain the steady acquisition pipeline underneath it.

Sources: PR Newswire via Finviz; Smart Water Magazine; Zacks

DesalinationMiddle East

Veolia adds two Gulf desalination contracts worth nearly €200 million

Veolia won a design contract for the 410,000 cubic meter a day Al Hamriyah plant in Sharjah, which will supply about 1.4 million people, and a five-year contract to operate and optimize the 80,000 cubic meter a day Al Asilah plant in Oman. The French group announced both on November 5.

Why it matters. Operations contracts on existing plants are a growing share of Gulf desalination spending, alongside new build. They give technology owners recurring revenue after construction ends.

Sources: Water Online; Smart Water Magazine

DesalinationMexico

Cox wins the construction contract for Mexico's Rosarito desalination plant

Mexico's National Water Commission awarded the Rosarito construction contract to Cox for about $304 million, after two earlier tenders were declared void. The plant is designed for 2,200 liters a second, about 190,000 cubic meters a day, within a project Conagua has put at $840 million in federal and state investment.

Why it matters. Baja California depends heavily on the Colorado River. A large coastal plant adds a supply source that does not ride on river allocations.

Sources: Mexico Business News; Smart Water Magazine

RegulationUnited States

Utilities still wait on EPA's final PFAS rule

EPA proposed in May to keep the 2024 drinking water limits for PFOA and PFOS while giving qualifying systems two more years, to 2031, to comply, and to rescind limits for four other PFAS. The comment period closed July 20. The agency has said it plans to finalize the changes in 2026.

Why it matters. The compliance date drives treatment capital plans for thousands of systems. Until the final rule lands, utilities are budgeting against two possible deadlines.

Sources: EPA; Bloomberg Law; Congressional Research Service

Supplier of the weekWeek of November 16

Treatment chemistry and digital water

Ecolab's water business is being pulled by chips and AI data centers

Ecolab reported third quarter sales up 13% to $4.69 billion on October 27, with organic growth accelerating to 7%. Chairman and chief executive Christophe Beck called the quarter an inflection point, and the water segment is where the change shows most clearly.

Global Water sales rose 20% at fixed currency to $2.48 billion. Eleven points came from the Ovivo Electronics and CoolIT acquisitions, and the rest from organic growth led by Global High-Tech, which grew 63% organically on semiconductor and data center demand.

The cost of that bet is visible too. CoolIT amortization and financing took roughly 10% off adjusted earnings growth, holding adjusted EPS to $2.18, up 5%. Management guides full-year adjusted EPS to $8.10 to $8.20.

Sources: Ecolab Form 8-K; Zacks; Quartr

Twenty five countries, home to one quarter of the world's population, use more than 80% of their renewable water supply every year. Even a short drought puts them at risk of running out.

Demand keeps climbing as the world adds two billion people

Global water demand has more than doubled since 1960 and has grown by just under 1% a year since the 1980s. The UN expects the population to rise from 8.2 billion in 2024 to a peak of about 10.3 billion in the mid 2080s.
