Drought and supply

Durham has paid over $4 million for borrowed water as North Carolina's drought reaches 6.2 million people

With 76 days of easily accessible supply left, the city is buying treated water from Cary and OWASA while it waits years for its own Jordan Lake intake.

North Carolina's drought deepened again this week. The state's November 19 report put 39 counties in central and eastern North Carolina in extreme drought and 17 more in severe drought, and the state estimates 6.2 million people, more than half the population, live in drought affected areas. As of November 17, Durham had 76 days of easily accessible water supply, according to the Department of Environmental Quality.

Durham has been buying water since it declared a drought on June 15. Invoices obtained by WRAL show 852 million gallons from the Town of Cary and 46 million gallons from the Orange Water and Sewer Authority, for a combined $4.16 million through October, before any November bills. Durham holds a 16.5 million gallon a day allocation in Jordan Lake but has no intake or plant of its own there, so it pays Cary to treat and deliver it. Cary says it is near its transfer capacity.

The city is under Stage 2 restrictions, which ban sprinkler irrigation and ask businesses using more than 100,000 gallons a day to cut 30%. Stage 3 would follow if forecasts show at least a 10% chance that combined storage falls to 25% within eight weeks. The long term fix, a Jordan Lake intake and a plant that could expand to 40 million gallons a day, is expected in the 2030 to 2032 timeframe. Durham says the purchases are paid from rates and should not weigh much on future increases.

Sources: NC Department of Environmental Quality; WRAL; ABC11; WUNC; WRAL

Down the pipe

What this story means at each stage of the water cycle

Source

Lake Michie and Little River Reservoir are well below full, and Falls Lake, Jordan Lake and Kerr Lake are all below normal.

Treat

Durham lacks its own Jordan Lake plant, so a neighbor's treatment capacity has become the bottleneck.

Use

Stage 2 bans sprinklers and asks large commercial users to cut 30%; Stage 3 would go further.

Reuse

Regional plans lean on new storage, such as OWASA's quarry reservoir from 2030, more than on reuse.

Discharge

Lower reservoirs leave less room for treated wastewater; Falls Lake is down to 12% of its water quality pool.

Use it

Utilities
Unused raw water allocations are worth little without intake and treatment to use them.
Investors
Drought costs land on operating budgets now and on capital plans later. Watch rate filings.
Industrial users
Plants above 100,000 gallons a day are first in line for mandatory cuts.
Technology firms
Interconnects, leak control and demand tools sell fastest when storage is falling.

Across the water economy

The deals, contracts and rules that moved since the last edition.

CapitalUnited States

American Water tells staff merger planning with Essential is largely complete

In a November 20 email filed with the SEC, American Water chief executive John Griffith told employees that organizational planning for the Essential Utilities merger is largely complete and that changes take effect only at closing, targeted for the end of the first quarter of 2027. Illinois became the fourth state to approve the all-stock deal on November 6, after Kentucky, Ohio and Virginia. Settlements are pending approval in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, North Carolina and Texas.

Why it matters. The combined company would serve more than 4.7 million water and wastewater connections. Four state decisions remain, and Illinois consumer advocates had asked for a five-year acquisition moratorium, a sign of the conditions other states may weigh.

Sources: SEC Form 425; Smart Water Magazine

UtilitiesUnited Kingdom

Thames Water's plan to push £12 billion of debt to 2028 goes to court on November 27

Thames Water has signed a support agreement with senior creditors to extend debt due in 2027 to March 2028, with interest still paid. Creditors holding more than £12 billion of secured debt have signed. A convening hearing is set for November 27, creditor meetings for December 17 and a sanction hearing for January 13, with the change effective around early February. The company says no decision has been made to accept the London & Valley Water creditor rescue proposal.

Why it matters. The extension buys time, not a solution. England's largest water company still carries more than £20 billion of debt, and the shape of any recapitalization will set terms for regulated water investment across the UK.

Sources: Thames Water; Yahoo Finance UK (PA)

RegulationUnited States

EWG's tap water update finds chromium-6 in water serving more than 260 million Americans

The Environmental Working Group's annual Tap Water Database update, released November 19 and built on 2024 and 2025 utility data from all 50 states and DC, reports hexavalent chromium in the drinking water of more than 260 million people. There is no federal limit for it. On PFAS, EPA proposed in May to keep 4 parts per trillion limits for PFOA and PFOS while letting systems request until 2031 to comply, and to rescind limits for PFHxS, PFNA, GenX and a mixture index.

Why it matters. Federal limits are narrowing while public data keeps expanding. Utilities face customer questions on contaminants with no federal standard, and treatment vendors face a longer, less certain PFAS compliance timeline.

Sources: Water Online (EWG release); US EPA

TechnologyIndia

Ecolab pilots zero wastewater discharge at chip plants and plans to bring it to India

Ecolab is developing pilot projects that cut industrial wastewater discharge to zero, mainly at semiconductor and electronics plants, Mukul Girotra, head of its global high tech division, told Business Standard on November 20. He said a decent sized fab uses about 8 million gallons of water a day and that the company now treats, recovers and reuses water from ultrapure water production. Ecolab wants to apply the work in India as the country builds its chip industry.

Why it matters. New fabs in water stressed regions are being judged on discharge as well as intake. Zero discharge moves spending from municipal supply to onsite treatment and chemistry contracts.

Sources: Business Standard

DesalinationUAE and Oman

Veolia wins nearly €200 million in Gulf desalination work

Veolia has won two contracts worth nearly €200 million combined, announced November 5. In Sharjah it will design and supply technology for the 410,000 cubic meter a day Al Hamriyah plant, serving about 1.4 million people, which it says will reach a record 2.75 kWh per cubic meter. In Oman it will operate and optimize the 80,000 cubic meter a day Al Asilah plant for five years through Bahwan Veolia Water.

Why it matters. Energy per cubic meter is now the main competitive metric in Gulf seawater reverse osmosis. Veolia aims to double its operated desalination capacity from 1.4 million cubic meters a day by 2030.

Sources: Saudi Gulf Projects; Water Online (Veolia release)

Supplier of the weekWeek of November 16

Treatment chemistry and digital water

Ecolab's water business is being pulled by chips and AI data centers

Ecolab reported third quarter sales up 13% to $4.69 billion on October 27, with organic growth accelerating to 7%. Chairman and chief executive Christophe Beck called the quarter an inflection point, and the water segment is where the change shows most clearly.

Global Water sales rose 20% at fixed currency to $2.48 billion. Eleven points came from the Ovivo Electronics and CoolIT acquisitions, and the rest from organic growth led by Global High-Tech, which grew 63% organically on semiconductor and data center demand.

The cost of that bet is visible too. CoolIT amortization and financing took roughly 10% off adjusted earnings growth, holding adjusted EPS to $2.18, up 5%. Management guides full-year adjusted EPS to $8.10 to $8.20.

Sources: Ecolab Form 8-K; Zacks; Quartr

Water place of the week

Week of November 16. One site where water engineering is worth seeing in person.

Singapore

Marina Barrage

A dam in the middle of a city that people come to picnic on

Marina Barrage closes the 350 meter wide Marina Channel with nine steel crest gates, keeping seawater out and turning the bay into Singapore's 15th reservoir and the first in the city center. Its catchment of about 10,000 hectares is the largest in the country, and the same structure protects low lying downtown streets from flooding.

The pump house is topped by a grass roof of about 11,000 square meters, open around the clock, where people fly kites, picnic and watch the skyline beside a solar installation. National water agency PUB counted close to 15 million visitors in the barrage's first ten years.

2008Opened, October 31
10,000 haCatchment, Singapore's largest
9Crest gates, each 26.5 m wide

Why it matters. It is the clearest example anywhere of water infrastructure that pays a second dividend: supply, flood control and a public space in one asset.

If you go. Free shuttle bus from Marina Bay MRT station. The green roof and public areas are open 24 hours.

Sources: PUB, National Library Board Singapore, National Archives of Singapore, PUB visitor FAQ, Our Future Water
