Data centers and supply

Nevada's data center boom could need about 34,000 acre-feet of water a year, most of it at power plants

An updated Desert Research Institute study puts direct cooling demand near 12,800 acre-feet a year by 2036. The water consumed to generate their electricity is far larger.

The Desert Research Institute has revised its January estimate of what data centers will mean for water in Nevada, the driest state. The update uses NV Energy's 2026 planning forecast, which lists 39 proposed high load data centers requesting 16,530 megawatts by 2036. The January study covered 12 projects and about 5,900 megawatts.

At the midpoint of its scenarios, the institute estimates direct cooling use of about 12,770 acre-feet a year, roughly 4 billion gallons, plus about 21,283 acre-feet consumed at the utility level to generate the power. Data centers are about 5% of NV Energy's sales today and are expected to reach 64% by 2046, according to the Nevada Independent.

The supply question is where the business case sits. The report says new growth will largely come from unappropriated groundwater, water reclamation or water transfers, and that direct demand will depend on cooling technology, with direct to chip and immersion systems among the more efficient options. A closed loop 100 megawatt facility in Las Vegas used less than 2 acre-feet in 2025, while an evaporative Google site in Henderson used about 636 acre-feet in 2024.

Sources: Facilities Dive; The Nevada Independent; Desert Research Institute, January report

Down the pipe

What this story means at each stage of the water cycle

Source

New demand will lean on unappropriated groundwater, reclaimed water and transfers in a state with little surface water to spare.

Treat

Reclaimed and brackish sources mean more treatment ahead of cooling systems and more brine to manage.

Use

Cooling design decides direct use: closed loop sites can use almost nothing, evaporative sites hundreds of acre-feet.

Reuse

Reclamation is one of the three named supply routes, which puts municipal effluent in play as an industrial asset.

Discharge

Blowdown from evaporative cooling concentrates salts and has to go somewhere in a closed desert basin.

Use it

Investors
Grid water now outweighs cooling water. Power siting is a water risk, not just an energy one.
Utilities
Reclaimed water contracts with data center developers can become a revenue line.
Data center operators
Closed loop and direct to chip cooling shrink the permit fight in arid states.
Technology firms
Brackish treatment, reuse and low water cooling are the products Nevada buyers will need.

Across the water economy

The deals, contracts and rules that moved since the last edition.

CapitalUnited States

California Water Service becomes Watermark Utilities and heads for Nevada and Oregon

California Water Service Group will trade under the name Watermark Utilities from November 30, keeping the CWT ticker, according to an 8-K filed November 18. The company, which serves more than 2.2 million people through subsidiaries in California, Hawaii, New Mexico, Texas and Washington, says it plans to expand into Nevada and Oregon before year end.

Why it matters. A new name that drops the home state signals a multi-state acquirer. It arrives as American Water and Essential pursue their merger and small system purchases keep the consolidation pipeline full.

Sources: SEC Form 8-K via StockTitan

DesalinationSaudi Arabia

Saudi Water Authority cancels WABAG's $272 million Yanbu desalination award

The Saudi Water Authority cancelled the tender for a 300 million liter a day seawater reverse osmosis plant at Yanbu, citing internal reasons, VA Tech Wabag told Indian exchanges on November 14. WABAG had signed the contract awarded in July 2025 and sent it for counter-signature, which never came. It was the second award of the project to the company, after one in September 2024 at about $317 million.

Why it matters. Gulf desalination awards are not final until the buyer signs. Contractors carrying Saudi projects in their order books may face closer questions from investors. The authority has not said whether it will re-tender.

Sources: Saudi Gulf Projects

UtilitiesTexas

Texas regulators hold off on Aqua Texas rates and weigh a five-year phase-in

The Public Utility Commission of Texas ended a November 19 hearing on Aqua Texas rates without a decision. Commissioners questioned the utility's records and asked staff to calculate rates under the administrative law judges' recommendations. Options include lower rates on those recommendations, a five-year phase-in that staff had proposed, or sending the case back for more review. Aqua Texas says its recordkeeping has improved.

Why it matters. Rate shock is becoming a political limit on how fast investor-owned utilities recover capital spending. A ruling could come at an open meeting as soon as December.

Sources: KPRC Click2Houston

EnergyPermian Basin

Select Water closes its $700 million purchase of Pilot Water Solutions

Select Water Solutions completed its acquisition of Delaware Basin produced water company Pilot Water Solutions on November 2, paying about $600 million in cash and 5.1 million shares. Pilot brings about 2.7 million barrels a day of active permitted disposal capacity and more than 700 miles of pipeline. Select funded the deal with $900 million of 7.625% senior notes due 2034.

Why it matters. Produced water is now a midstream business financed like one. Select expects the combined company to handle more than 2.5 million barrels a day in 2027.

Sources: Smart Water Magazine

UtilitiesAustralia

TasWater's new sewer pricing cuts hospital charges about 70% while some customers faced 500% rises

Tasmania's water utility moved some non-residential fixed sewerage charges to a basis of water connection size from July 1. Health department documents show Launceston General Hospital's fixed charge fell to $12,531 for July to September from $67,361 a year earlier. Customers with large connections, such as sports grounds, were initially set for increases of up to 500%, and TasWater paused those bills on October 2. The utility says it still expects to collect its planned revenue.

Why it matters. Tariff redesign shifts who pays without changing what a utility collects. Utilities moving to capacity based charges should model the outliers before bills go out.

Sources: ABC News Australia

Supplier of the weekWeek of November 16

Treatment chemistry and digital water

Ecolab's water business is being pulled by chips and AI data centers

Ecolab reported third quarter sales up 13% to $4.69 billion on October 27, with organic growth accelerating to 7%. Chairman and chief executive Christophe Beck called the quarter an inflection point, and the water segment is where the change shows most clearly.

Global Water sales rose 20% at fixed currency to $2.48 billion. Eleven points came from the Ovivo Electronics and CoolIT acquisitions, and the rest from organic growth led by Global High-Tech, which grew 63% organically on semiconductor and data center demand.

The cost of that bet is visible too. CoolIT amortization and financing took roughly 10% off adjusted earnings growth, holding adjusted EPS to $2.18, up 5%. Management guides full-year adjusted EPS to $8.10 to $8.20.

Sources: Ecolab Form 8-K; Zacks; Quartr

Water place of the week

Week of November 16. One site where water engineering is worth seeing in person.

Singapore

Marina Barrage

A dam in the middle of a city that people come to picnic on

Marina Barrage closes the 350 meter wide Marina Channel with nine steel crest gates, keeping seawater out and turning the bay into Singapore's 15th reservoir and the first in the city center. Its catchment of about 10,000 hectares is the largest in the country, and the same structure protects low lying downtown streets from flooding.

The pump house is topped by a grass roof of about 11,000 square meters, open around the clock, where people fly kites, picnic and watch the skyline beside a solar installation. National water agency PUB counted close to 15 million visitors in the barrage's first ten years.

2008Opened, October 31
10,000 haCatchment, Singapore's largest
9Crest gates, each 26.5 m wide

Why it matters. It is the clearest example anywhere of water infrastructure that pays a second dividend: supply, flood control and a public space in one asset.

If you go. Free shuttle bus from Marina Bay MRT station. The green roof and public areas are open 24 hours.

Sources: PUB, National Library Board Singapore, National Archives of Singapore, PUB visitor FAQ, Our Future Water
