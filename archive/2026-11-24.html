Produced water and land

Texas Pacific Land agrees to buy EagleRock for about $3 billion in stock to widen its Permian water platform

The all-stock deal adds 59,000 acres in Lea County, New Mexico and Midland Basin water assets, lifting combined sourcing and treatment capacity to about 1.5 million barrels a day.

Texas Pacific Land said on November 23 it will acquire EagleRock Land in an all-stock deal with an implied equity value of about $3 billion. EagleRock holders get 0.0831 shares of a new TPL holding company for each Class A share, about $23.56 based on TPL's five-day average price to November 20. TPL holders will own about 86% of the combined company. Reuters reported EagleRock shares up about 16% before the open.

Water is a large part of the logic. EagleRock brings about 220,000 barrels a day of produced water handling across gathering, disposal and recycling, plus about 130,000 barrels a day of non-potable supply, much of it tied to long-term arrangements with Double Eagle covering about 105,000 dedicated acres. Combined, TPL would have about 1.5 million barrels a day of non-potable sourcing and produced water treatment, up 49%, and about 68 million barrels of storage, up 79%.

The deal also adds about 59,000 fee-owned acres, mostly in Lea County, which the companies say accounts for about 25% of Permian horizontal well permits and 20% of completion water demand. Chief executive Tyler Glover said it reinforces a "high-margin, capital-light business model." Closing is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to an EagleRock shareholder vote and antitrust clearance; holders of more than 50% of EagleRock's voting power have agreed to support it.

Sources: Business Wire via BOE Report, Nov 23, 2026; World Oil, Nov 23, 2026; Reuters via BOE Report, Nov 23, 2026

CapitalUnited States

California Water Service places $275 million of debt a week before becoming Watermark Utilities

California Water Service Group said on November 23 it finalized private placements of $125 million of senior unsecured notes, at 6.08% due 2031 and 6.41% due 2036, and its Cal Water subsidiary sold $150 million of first mortgage bonds at 6.49% due 2046 and 6.54% due 2056. Proceeds will refinance existing debt and fund general corporate purposes. The group serves more than 2.2 million people in five states and takes the name Watermark Utilities on November 30.

Why it matters. Long dated utility debt is pricing above 6%, a cost that flows into future rate cases. The company's own risk factors list the EPA PFAS limits and their legal challenges.

Sources: GlobeNewswire, Nov 23, 2026

RegulationGlobal

UN General Assembly agrees to hold a water conference every three years from 2031

The General Assembly adopted by consensus on November 23 a resolution led by the UAE and Senegal that sets periodic UN water conferences, with governments meeting every three years from 2031 after the agreed 2026 and 2028 meetings. The 2026 conference runs December 8 to 10 in Abu Dhabi, the first devoted to the sanitation and water goal, SDG 6. Before 2023, the UN had not held one since 1977.

Why it matters. A fixed cycle gives water a standing place on the global agenda. About 2.1 billion people still lacked safely managed drinking water in 2024, and the World Bank puts the scarcity cost to the Middle East and North Africa at 6 to 14% of GDP by 2050.

Sources: The National, Nov 23, 2026

SecurityChina and India

Satellite images show river diversion under way at China's 60 GW Yarlung Tsangpo dam near India

Business Today, citing a Times of India report based on EarthSense Labs imagery, said on November 24 that villages at the proposed Mainling site were cleared by December 2025 and that river diversion works, roads and housing appeared by August 2026. The Medog project, estimated at 1.2 trillion yuan, about $168 billion, is planned as five cascade stations on the river that becomes the Siang in India. India's proposed 11,200 MW Siang Upper project, with a reservoir of about 9.2 billion cubic meters, faces local opposition over displacement.

Why it matters. There is no binding water sharing treaty between the two countries, so the Brahmaputra basin is one of the largest transboundary water risks in Asia.

Sources: Business Today, Nov 24, 2026

SecurityUnited States

Free cyber defense for rural water utilities now reaches 26 states

The Water Watch Center, launched in August by the National Rural Water Association and the volunteer group DEF CON Franklin, pays security firms including Rapid7 and BlastWave to give small utilities free intrusion detection and consultations. It said utilities in 26 states are now covered and nearly 100 have reached out. Funders include Craig Newmark Philanthropies, the Hewlett Foundation and DARPA.

Why it matters. Iran linked hackers breached water systems in several US states this summer, and most small utilities have no security staff.

Sources: Cybersecurity Dive, Nov 23, 2026

UtilitiesUnited States

San Antonio eases to Stage 2 watering rules as the Edwards Aquifer recovers

San Antonio Water System said on November 23 it is moving from Stage 3 to Stage 2 restrictions after the Edwards Aquifer's 10-day average rose above the 640 foot Stage 3 trigger. Drought surcharges on single family use above 20,000 gallons a month end, and lawn watering stays limited to once a week by address.

Why it matters. Drought surcharges are a real revenue line for Texas utilities, and their end shows how quickly aquifer levels can swing rate and demand planning.

Sources: KSAT, Nov 23, 2026

Treatment chemistry and digital water

Ecolab's water business is being pulled by chips and AI data centers

Ecolab reported third quarter sales up 13% to $4.69 billion on October 27, with organic growth accelerating to 7%. Chairman and chief executive Christophe Beck called the quarter an inflection point, and the water segment is where the change shows most clearly.

Global Water sales rose 20% at fixed currency to $2.48 billion. Eleven points came from the Ovivo Electronics and CoolIT acquisitions, and the rest from organic growth led by Global High-Tech, which grew 63% organically on semiconductor and data center demand.

The cost of that bet is visible too. CoolIT amortization and financing took roughly 10% off adjusted earnings growth, holding adjusted EPS to $2.18, up 5%. Management guides full-year adjusted EPS to $8.10 to $8.20.

Week of November 23. One site where water engineering is worth seeing in person.

Istanbul, Türkiye

Basilica Cistern

A sixth century reservoir held up by a forest of 336 columns

Built between 532 and 542 under the Eastern Roman emperor Justinian I, the Basilica Cistern, Yerebatan Sarnıcı in Turkish, was one of the city's major water stores, with an estimated capacity of about 100,000 tons. Its roof rests on 336 columns, each 9 meters tall.

Two carved Medusa heads, identified in 1986, serve as column bases, one upside down and one on its side. The cistern opened to visitors in 1987 after a major restoration, and reopened on July 22, 2022 after a second restoration that began in 2014.

Why it matters. Istanbul hosts the IWRA World Water Congress in October 2027, and this is the city's oldest lesson in storing water close to demand.

If you go. In Istanbul's historic peninsula; open as a museum. Check opening hours and tickets before you go.
