Data centers and water

Nevada's planned data centers could use 4 billion gallons a year to cool, and power plants nearly twice that

A Desert Research Institute study of 39 proposed sites in the driest US state finds the bigger water draw is the electricity, not the cooling towers.

The Desert Research Institute released an updated study on November 18 of 39 proposed high load data centers in Nevada that together have requested 16,530 megawatts. By 2036 they could add 43,754 gigawatt hours of electricity demand. Direct water use for cooling would reach about 12,770 acre-feet a year, roughly 4 billion gallons, according to Facilities Dive's summary of the report.

The larger figure is indirect. Generating the power for those sites would consume about 21,283 acre-feet of water a year, close to twice the cooling demand. The study assumes a midpoint of 0.36 liters per kilowatt hour for on site water use and uses NV Energy's 2026 estimates, which reflect a 180% jump in data center capacity requests since 2024. It says new supply would largely have to come from unappropriated groundwater, water reclamation or water transfers.

A federal audit released the same day points the same way. The Government Accountability Office found that none of 21 agencies running 1,392 federal data centers fully met all five water and energy planning requirements, that cooling can take up to 40% of a facility's energy, and that the requirements lapsed on September 30. GAO asked Congress to consider reauthorizing or replacing the law.

Sources: Facilities Dive, Nov 20, 2026; Western Water on GAO-27-108971, Nov 20, 2026

Down the pipe

What this story means at each stage of the water cycle

Source

Nevada averages about 10.3 inches of rain a year; new supply would come from unappropriated groundwater, reclamation or transfers.

Treat

Reclaimed and brackish sources need treatment before they can stand in for potable supply in cooling systems.

Use

About 12,770 acre-feet a year for cooling by 2036, and about 21,283 acre-feet at the power plants serving them.

Reuse

Water reclamation is one of the three supply routes the study names for new growth.

Discharge

The study's figures are consumption estimates; discharge and blowdown are not broken out in the coverage.

Use it

Utilities
Price and plan for the power plant water behind a data center, not just the site meter.
Investors
Water rights, reclamation and transfers in the Southwest gain value as load requests climb.
Industrial users
Large new loads compete for the same groundwater and reclaimed water you may need.
Technology firms
Low water cooling and reuse treatment have a measurable target: 0.36 liters per kWh today.

CapitalUnited States

Diamondback and KKR buy out Five Point to own all of the Deep Blue water network

Diamondback Energy and KKR agreed on November 10 to buy Five Point Infrastructure's 70% stake in Deep Blue Midland Basin, giving the two full ownership; Diamondback already held 30%. Terms were not disclosed, but Bloomberg reported an equity value of about $2.1 billion in cash. Deep Blue runs more than 2,000 miles of produced water pipe in the Midland Basin. KKR is investing mainly through its Global Infrastructure Investors V fund, and closing is expected in the fourth quarter.

Why it matters. It is the second large Permian water deal this month, after Select Water's $700 million purchase of Pilot, and Diamondback named industrial uses beyond oil and gas, including data center cooling, as growth options.

Sources: Oil & Gas Journal, Nov 10, 2026; Bloomberg via Energy Connects; World Oil, Nov 12, 2026

SecurityFiji

Fiji declares a nationwide state of disaster as El Niño drought reaches crisis level

Fiji's Cabinet approved a nationwide state of disaster on November 18 on the advice of the National Disaster Risk Management Council, and Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka announced it in a national address. The decree allows water rationing, evacuations and reallocation of funds, and Reuters reported FJ$20 million redeployed for emergency water to households and farmers. Forecasters expect below normal rainfall into early 2027.

Why it matters. Tonga has also declared a drought crisis and Papua New Guinea is scaling up aid for at least 680,000 people, so Pacific island water supply is becoming a regional emergency.

Sources: Nature World News, Nov 19, 2026

TechnologyUnited States

Chip stocks whipsaw over Nvidia's leadership, testing the AI build-out behind data center water demand

Nvidia shares fell 14% on Friday, November 20, and the S&P 500 closed in a correction, extending a selloff that followed co-founder Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO on November 12. On Sunday, President Trump posted that he hoped Nvidia's board would rehire Huang. Nvidia rose about 9% in premarket trading Monday and Nasdaq futures gained about 3%. As of Monday morning the board had not announced a decision; CFO Colette Kress remains interim CEO.

Why it matters. Data center and chip fab water demand, including the Nevada pipeline in today's lead, rides on AI capital spending. Watch whether cloud providers hold their build-out plans; suppliers such as Ecolab and Kurita sell water treatment into that cycle.

Sources: Yahoo Finance, Nov 23, 2026; Yahoo Finance markets live, Nov 23, 2026; The San Francisco Standard, Nov 12, 2026

DesalinationSaudi Arabia

Saudi Water Authority cancels Wabag's $272 million Yanbu desalination award, again

VA Tech Wabag told Indian exchanges on November 14 that the Saudi Water Authority had cancelled the tender for a 300,000 cubic meter a day seawater reverse osmosis plant at Yanbu, citing internal reasons. Wabag had signed the roughly $272 million contract and was waiting for the authority's countersignature. It is the second cancellation: a first award in September 2024 was withdrawn that December before the project was re-awarded in July 2025.

Why it matters. Saudi procurement keeps shifting. Contractors pricing Gulf work should weigh retender risk, and Wabag shares fell about 7% on November 16.

Sources: Smart Water Magazine, Nov 17, 2026; EquityBulls, Nov 14, 2026

CapitalUnited States

Air Water Ventures agrees to buy Proud Source Water for up to $150 million

Nasdaq listed Air Water Ventures agreed on November 17 to acquire Proud Source Water, a Florida bottled water brand, for $5 million in cash and stock with a reference value of $95 million at closing, plus up to $50 million tied to future performance. The deal includes a Florida plant and water rights running through 2051. Proud Source reported about $30.4 million of preliminary 2025 revenue.

Why it matters. Long dated spring water rights are the core asset in bottled water deals, and closing is not expected until 2027, subject to shareholder and regulatory approval.

Sources: FoodBev Media, Nov 19, 2026

Supplier of the weekWeek of November 16

Treatment chemistry and digital water

Ecolab's water business is being pulled by chips and AI data centers

Ecolab reported third quarter sales up 13% to $4.69 billion on October 27, with organic growth accelerating to 7%. Chairman and chief executive Christophe Beck called the quarter an inflection point, and the water segment is where the change shows most clearly.

Global Water sales rose 20% at fixed currency to $2.48 billion. Eleven points came from the Ovivo Electronics and CoolIT acquisitions, and the rest from organic growth led by Global High-Tech, which grew 63% organically on semiconductor and data center demand.

The cost of that bet is visible too. CoolIT amortization and financing took roughly 10% off adjusted earnings growth, holding adjusted EPS to $2.18, up 5%. Management guides full-year adjusted EPS to $8.10 to $8.20.

Water place of the week

Week of November 23. One site where water engineering is worth seeing in person.

Istanbul, Türkiye

Basilica Cistern

A sixth century reservoir held up by a forest of 336 columns

Built between 532 and 542 under the Eastern Roman emperor Justinian I, the Basilica Cistern, Yerebatan Sarnıcı in Turkish, was one of the city's major water stores, with an estimated capacity of about 100,000 tons. Its roof rests on 336 columns, each 9 meters tall.

Two carved Medusa heads, identified in 1986, serve as column bases, one upside down and one on its side. The cistern opened to visitors in 1987 after a major restoration, and reopened on July 22, 2022 after a second restoration that began in 2014.

Why it matters. Istanbul hosts the IWRA World Water Congress in October 2027, and this is the city's oldest lesson in storing water close to demand.

If you go. In Istanbul's historic peninsula; open as a museum. Check opening hours and tickets before you go.

Sources: Daily Sabah, Nov 18, 2026
